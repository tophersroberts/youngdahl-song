As The Brush Goes · Reading 26

Losing my hometown

It was the place I had to abandon and
leave behind when love was cooling down.
Swearing I never want to see it again.

But now that
the shadows on the ground keep getting
longer and the silence around
me deepens, how can I bear to want to go back?

The journey back to my hometown was not easy. In the
meantime, both the roads that didn’t exist before and
those that were being built were somehow different from the past.
The houses where people live are different,
and the various shops and signs have also changed.

The biggest change is the people.

The face, the name, and the language used have all changed.
It seems my face, name, and language have changed in the meantime.
Even the people from my hometown don’t recognize me right away.

The hometown I am now leaving behind has forsaken me,
and I have become a wanderer who has lost my hometown.

Becoming one of the countless diaspora, partly by choice and partly by
circumstance, and looking at the white clouds outside the
airplane window, I become a pale traveler with a white
face, even though I am not white.
