As The Brush Goes · Reading 27

A thought on a winter night

I do not let this winter abandon me,
I hope not to lose this winter.

Even though snow has piled up, camellias are blooming red in the garden,
Even though all the leaves have fallen, the roof has been newly thatched.

While skimming off the thin ice and taking out the white radish
Slice it thinly, make dongchimi, and gulp it down.

If by any chance one late night feels lonely
Roasting chestnuts over a hearth fire and flipping them with metal chopsticks

Sharing stories of sweating through that sweltering summer,
I want to love this winter for a very, very long time.

I do not wait for a spring that hesitates and is reluctant to approach.
I would even turn down a spring where I want to hug tightly and fly with a butterfly.

They say spring goes and spring comes again, but
since that might be someone else’s spring, not mine, even
though my hands and feet are cold and my back aches,
I want to spend this winter savoring it for a long, long time.

Now well over eighty, I hope that
this winter does not abandon me,
and that I do not lose a winter I will never see again.
